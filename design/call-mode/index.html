Call Mode, Five Ways

Five mockups of the calling screen in the TaylorURL console, drawn in the console's own ink. They answer the same four questions and disagree about which of them belongs in the middle of the screen. They are flat HTML and nothing on them is wired to anything.

What All Five Have To Answer

A Number To Aim At

Call Mode counts nothing but what is left, which is four thousand today and four thousand tomorrow. A caller cannot have a good morning against a figure like that.

A Reason To Keep Going

Pace, said as a clock time rather than a rate. Nobody converts calls a minute in their head while a phone is ringing.

Words To Say

The handbook is a search field in an overlay three columns from the business it is about. The opening line belongs on the card.

The Calls Before This One

Most businesses are rung four or five times. Today that reaches the caller as one clause, and who to ask for is in a note nobody opens mid-call.

One: The Shift

A rail across the top holding three figures the caller agreed to before picking up the phone. The follow-up sits above the business name, where the caller reads it before composing an opener.

Goals Take The Top Script In Full

Two: Script First

The words take the card. The beat being said is set large, the beats behind it fall back to their labels, and the outcomes stay pinned to the foot so a call never ends by hunting for a button.

Script Takes The Middle Outcomes Pinned

Three: The Record

Every call to this business drawn as a column, with the one about to be placed as the last entry in it, still empty. The wait the ladder bought is written into the gap between two entries.

History Takes The Middle Goals As Rings

Four: The Coach

A rail down the right that follows the call rather than sitting still. The board counts the shift by the hour, so being behind names the hour it happened in. This one opens on a time the owner named herself.

Answers Take A Column Hour By Hour

Five: One Call

The least that can be on screen while a phone is ringing. The shift is a rule, the batch is a strip of marks, and the figures come back in full where the batch runs out.
