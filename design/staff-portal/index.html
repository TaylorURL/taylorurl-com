The staff system

Thumb is the direction. It is drawn at both sizes below, and the five explorations it came out of are underneath that. Flat HTML on the site's own tokens; only the applied call screen runs.

The direction Thumb, applied

A head that is only read, one scrolling column, and a foot that never moves - on a phone beside the handset and on a desktop, drawn side by side so they cannot become two designs. All four surfaces, plus the screen a list runs out on. The call screen runs: the tags select, Needs Time opens its lengths, Next unlocks once both questions are answered, and it moves between two businesses.

The four surfaces, in every system

Portal 01

The gate, and the way to the other three. Main Site, Call Center, Management Center, Resources Center.

Call Center 02

The only surface staff drive. One business at a time: the number, who they are, every call before this one, the words to say, and the two questions once the handset is down.

Management Center 03

Who is on the phones, how far through their list, what came back today, and the two things only a manager can do: move a list and read the refusals.

Resources Center 04

The script, the prices, the answers to pushback and the written procedures. Read once in the first week and searched forever after.

Two questions at the end of a call, not one

What the call came to is about the call: no answer, voicemail, gatekeeper, spoke to owner, booked, wrong number. What the business is is about next year: Yes, Maybe, Needs Time, Price Issue, No. A phone that rang out is not a business that said no, so one field cannot carry both. Needs Time is the only tag that opens anything, and it offers four lengths rather than a date, because the only thing read off it later is which month to put the business back in.

The five it came out of

Each draws all four surfaces, because the decisions worth arguing about are the ones between them: whether this is one application or four, where the handbook sits when somebody is on the line, and whether anybody can see anybody else.

Full Bleed 01

Four products that share a login.

No rail, no tabs, no chrome carried between screens. Each surface is the whole window doing one job, which buys size: the number is set to be read into a handset and the script to be read out loud.

Shape
Four separate full-screen pages
Handbook
Its own screen, a page away
Best for

 The Shell 02

One application with four rooms.

A rail that never leaves and a panel that changes with the room. It costs 15rem of every screen and buys the handbook being one click away with somebody on the line, which is the difference between reading a price and guessing it.

Shape
Persistent rail, room, context panel
Handbook
Beside the call, always open
Best for
New representatives, and switching often
 Key First 03

Nothing needs a mouse.

Sixty calls is a hundred and eighty trips to a pointer. Every control carries the key that fires it, in the same place on every screen, and the whole thing is drawn on a dark slab because somebody looks at it for eight hours.

Shape
Dense, dark, three columns, no scroll
Handbook
A palette, reached by slash mid-call
Best for
A floor doing this full time
 Thumb 04 · picked

Built for the device actually in the room.

A phone or tablet propped beside the handset, worked with one thumb while the other hand writes down a name. One column, one scroll, and a bar at the foot that never moves. The same shape carries all four surfaces.

Shape
One column, fixed foot, 56px controls
Handbook
Folded topics, the call still open behind
Best for
Calling away from a desk
 The Floor 05

Everybody can see everybody.

Three people do this at the same time and every screen says where the others are. Visible standing rather than ranking, and nobody is last: a board that ranks people is one somebody games by hanging up early.

Shape
A strip of the floor on all four screens
Handbook
Shared, and the floor writes to it
Best for
Three or more people calling together
