Opening the call

The person answering a shop's phone is not the person who buys a website. A caller who opens with the pitch has spent it on somebody who cannot say yes, so the first line asks for the owner and nothing else.

The four beats, in order

  1. Ask for the owner by name. If you do not have a name, ask who owns it.
  2. Say who you are and where you are calling from, then say you are not selling anything over the phone. That sentence buys the next twenty seconds.
  3. Say what is wrong with their listing. Be specific about what you saw, because that is what proves you looked them up rather than bought a list.
  4. Ask for ten minutes this week. A day and a time, never soon and never sometime.

When they push back

We already have a Facebook page.
That is where most of your customers found you, and it is the one thing you do not own. A site is the part nobody can take down.
How much is it.
From $1,000 to build and from $250 a month after. The number is agreed before any work starts, so there is nothing to find out later.
Send me something.
I can, and I would rather take ten minutes first so what I send is about your shop rather than a brochure. What does Tuesday look like?
I am too busy for new work.
Then you are the one this is worth least to today and most to in February. It costs nothing to have it ready before the quiet stretch.

Procedure: an owner says yes on the call

  1. Mark the call Booked and tag the business Yes before you hang up.
  2. Get the email address and read it back to them letter by letter. A wrong address is a booking that never happened.
  3. Put the day and the time in the note. Not a range, and not this week.
  4. The plan and the price go out the same day, from Trenton.

Procedure: somebody asks to never be called again

  1. Say you will take them off now, and mean it. Do not ask why.
  2. Tag the business No. That takes it off every list for good.
  3. Write what they said in the note, in their words.
