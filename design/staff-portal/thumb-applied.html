Thumb, applied

One shape at both sizes. A head that is only read, one scrolling column, and a foot that never moves, so the control that ends a call is in the same place on the sixtieth call as on the first. The desktop is the same three parts with the column held at a reading measure and the bars run full width.

The call screen runs

Press Mark This Called and the two questions arrive. Needs Time opens its lengths. Next unlocks once both are answered, and it moves to a second business - a first call, with no record and no website, which is the other script and the empty state in one row. Back returns. Nothing is stored and nothing is sent.

When They Push Back

We already have a Facebook page.

That is where most of your customers found you, and it is the one thing you do not own. A site is the part nobody can take down.

How much is it.

From $1,000 to build and from $250 a month after. The number is agreed before any work starts, so there is nothing to find out later.

Send me something.

I can, and I would rather take ten minutes first so what I send is about your shop rather than a brochure. What does Tuesday look like?

My nephew is building me one.

Good. Ask him who the domain is registered to, and whether anyone is watching it after it goes up. Those are the two that bite a year later.

Opening The Call

The person answering a shop's phone is not the person who buys a website. Ask for the owner first and spend nothing on anybody who cannot say yes.

  1. Ask for the owner by name. If you have no name, ask who owns it.
  2. Say who you are, then say you are not selling anything over the phone.
  3. Say what is wrong with their listing, specifically.
  4. Ask for ten minutes this week. A day and a time, never soon.

Closing The Call

Ask for a day and a time. Soon and this week are not answers, and a meeting without a time is a meeting that does not happen.

Procedures

An owner says yes on the call

  1. Mark the call Booked and tag the business Yes before you hang up.
  2. Get the email and read it back letter by letter.
  3. Put the day and the time in the note. Not a range.
  4. The plan and the price go out the same day, from Trenton.

Somebody asks never to be called again

  1. Say you will take them off now, and mean it. Do not ask why.
  2. Tag the business No. That takes it off every list for good.
  3. Write what they said in the note, in their words.
Who To Escalate To

Anything about money, a refund, or a promise we have not made goes to Trenton before you answer it. Saying you will find out is never the wrong answer.
